# Interpretation notes (deferred from Progress Report 9)

Dated 2026-11-08. Tentative thoughts, not yet developed into the book — to be written up properly in `01-proposal.qmd`'s Expected Results and in the Discussion chapter once Lecture 10 is covered, per Progress Report 10.

- The significant positive relationship between income and rent is the expected direction (higher-income tracts command higher rent), consistent with the proposal's expectation — but a coefficient of ~$0.0047 of rent per dollar of income, with R² = 0.19, means income alone leaves most of the tract-to-tract variation in rent unexplained. Worth naming explicitly what else might matter: housing stock age/type, distance to downtown Albany, transit access — none of which this project currently measures.
- The spatial error finding (λ = 0.38, significant) is itself a substantive point, not just a technical correction: it means tracts near each other share rent levels for reasons the income variable alone doesn't capture — plausibly neighborhood effects, zoning, or amenities that cluster geographically. This is worth a sentence in Discussion about what a richer model (adding a neighborhood or submarket variable) might look like, without overclaiming causality from a two-variable cross-sectional regression.
- Only 2 of 82 tracts remained significant after FDR correction in the local Moran's I from Progress Report 8 — worth being honest that this is a modest, broad spatial tendency, not a small number of extreme "hot spot" tracts driving the whole result.
- The renter-vs-all-household income limitation (named in `01-proposal.qmd` since the proposal presentation) still applies here: the income variable may be overstating affordability if renters specifically earn less than the tract's overall household median. This doesn't change the direction of the main finding but could change its magnitude.
- Policy framing to avoid: nothing in this analysis supports a claim about *why* income and rent are related (market dynamics vs. discrimination vs. something else) — the data is observational and cross-sectional. Any discussion-chapter language should stick to "associated with," never "causes."
